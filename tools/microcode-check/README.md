# The microcode's checks

Cases that check the microcode assembled from `sys/ucadr/`, run on a band
through `tools/lispm-check`. Each check is a case file, `cases/NAME.cases`,
with the Lisp it needs first, if any, in `lisp/NAME.lisp`.

```
tools/microcode-check/run -- --band PACK --ubin UBIN              # every check
tools/microcode-check/run pdl-refill -- --band PACK --ubin UBIN   # one of them
tools/microcode-check/run pdl-refill -- --band PACK --ubin UBIN --engine rtl
```

`fixpack-flag.py` is a static check of the sources, with no band: every jump to
`FIXPACK-T` or `FIXPACK-P`, which test the fixnum overflow flag that every ALU
word loads, must have as its flag word (the jump's next word when it is
`-XCT-NEXT`, else the word before it) the arithmetic word that made `M-1`, or a
logical word, whose flag is 0; a call, a page-fault check, a dispatch, a byte
word or another register's sum between them breaks the rule. Exit status 0
when no site breaks it.

`rev14-mutants` assembles revision 14's microcode with one fault planted, for
`rev14-fiddles` and `rev14-paging` to be shown failing under it: the dump's
early A 430 and 431 copies removed, the dump's and the refill's status-6
recovery made `ILLOP-IF-PAGE-FAULT`, `UPDATE-REGION-PHT` clearing `<19>`, and
an eviction that takes every page for clean or writes every page
(`rev14-mutants --list`, its head says more). It assembles on a band through
lispm-check, as `tools/assembler-check` does.

The generational collector's microcode (contract G3 step 2, 9.1 items 1-10)
has the checks `generational` and `generational-2mw` below and three scripts.
`generational-static UCADR.SYM [QCOM.LISP]` is a static check with no band:
`A-GC-WORDS-CONSED-SINCE-FLIP`, `A-GC-PROMOTION` and `A-GC-PRETENURE-THRESHOLD`
at A 143-145, right after `A-REGION-FLOOR`, with `A-PDL-BUFFER-VIRTUAL-ADDRESS`
and `A-PDL-BUFFER-HEAD` still at A 430 and 431, and, given a qcom that names
them, `%REGION-FLOOR` and the three at the indexes of
`A-MEMORY-LOCATION-NAMES` that put them there. `generational-save` (item 8)
takes a disk whose LOD1 holds a band of System 2002 and whose MCR1 holds the
microcode under test, and works on a copy: a session makes young lists held
only by a static array's slot, by NIL's property list (a wired page) and,
through a `BEFORE-COLD` initialization, by a slot stored just before the save,
and saves the band; the script reads the band's mark bitmap from the
partition, checks its size and the holders' bits, and maps every 1 bit to its
page by the band's own region tables; the band, booted, must have every such
page marked, with no young flip since the save (`GS-NO-FLIP-SINCE-SAVE`: a
later flip's walk may clear a mark legitimately, so only then is a cleared
mark a lost one), the lists young, and their sums right after three young
collections; its head gives the arguments. With `--incremental` the save is
incremental (format 2021 over LOD1), and the checks that read the band's region
tables are left out. With `--engine rtl` each of its session's forms may take
ten times as long as on micro. Its session and its boot are
`tools/cross-build/run`'s, so their network follows the machine's revision
(QUUX's Ethernet contract, section 8 R2): over TCP on a tap on revision 16,
with no ozd, unless `LISPM_CHECK_NETWORK` says `chaos`, and over Chaos on
revisions 13-15 (`tools/cross-build/README.md`).
`generational-mutants` plants a partial fix of each item in a copy of the
microcode and assembles it, as `rev14-mutants` does (`--list`; `--src` for
another tree); the machine must then be given a disk with that microcode in
MCR1.

`boot-keys` checks the boot's choice of a cold or a warm boot at location 6
(`PROM`, `sys/ucadr/uc-cadr.lisp`) with words waiting in the keyboard's FIFO,
by booting the machine with no session and no network:

```
tools/microcode-check/boot-keys --out DIR --disk DISK --ubin UBIN --quux QUUX \
    --ports N [--engine micro|rtl] [CASE ...]
```

The keys are typed, on the glass TTY or the RFB terminal, while the machine
is held at muir's prompt (`--no-auto-boot`, or a checkpoint of the band at
its listener resumed and held), and then the prompt's `boot` presses the
button, so the words are in the FIFO before location 6 reads it whatever the
host's speed. The run stops at `BEG0000`: a warm boot reaches it within 3
million microcycles of the press on micro (location 6 is at 736 thousand), a
cold boot after the band's restore, 26.7 million; a second run goes on past
it and must not halt. The cases: `no-key`, `key` (a key typed, as at the
glass TTY's connect) and `rubout-power-on` (Control-Meta-Rubout) cold-boot
after power-on; `return-power-on` (Control-Meta-Return) warm-boots and then
halts at `ILLOP`, BEG0000's read of 4031 finding no band in memory, as MIT's
does; `return-running` warm-boots the band at its listener, and
`rubout-running` cold-boots it. MIT's test, one word read, takes `key` and
both Rubout cases for warm boots, the key-downs coming before the boot word:
on revision 16, micro, `key` and `rubout-power-on` halt at 32063 and
`rubout-running` warm-boots. Of two partial fixes, the test without the
FIFO's draining, the first word alone, fails `return-power-on` and
`return-running` (cold boots, Control's key-down first), and the draining
without the boot code's test fails both Rubout cases. On rtl the cold boot
reaches `BEG0000` 225 million microcycles of the run after the press, and a
halt at `HALT-CONS` is not reported, so `return-power-on`'s halt is recorded
and not judged there. DISK is copied and booted read-only; the exit status
is 0 when every case passes. Its head says more.

Arguments after `--` go to every lispm-check run. The machine runs the
microcode in the disk's MCR1, not the one in `ubin/`, so the disk must carry
the microcode under test: write `sys/ubin/ucadr.mcr` over MCR1, which starts
at block 17 (`dd if=ucadr.mcr of=DISK bs=1024 seek=17 conv=notrunc`), serve
the same `ubin/`, whose `ucadr.tbl` the cold boot reads, and give `quux` the
matching boot PROM (`--prom`, through a `--quux` wrapper; `docs/building.md`,
"A band of 1024-word pages for revision 12"). lispm-check's default band,
`run/check/band.img`, runs microcode 2000. The exit status is 0 when every
check passes.

The checks in `cases-15/` want revision 15 and run only when named, as
`cases-2mw/`'s do; named on another revision, each is skipped, `run` saying
so in one line, the machine (`--quux`) asked its revision as lispm-check asks
it. `network-stray` is there because step R4 of QUUX's Ethernet contract
(section 8) took the Chaosnet interface from revision 16, whose 140-157 are
reserved and whose word 100 `<6>` is never set.

The checks in `cases-16/` want revision 16, QUUX's Ethernet device behind
its words 240-257, and run only when named, as `cases-2mw/`'s do. The
device needs something behind it: `ethernet-peer SOCKET` listens on a UNIX
socket and sends every connection a broadcast ARP request every 20 ms, in
the plain mode of muir-sim's `--ethernet-socket` (QUUX's Ethernet contract,
section 3), so run it first and give the machine the socket through the
`--quux` wrapper, with the revision and the boot PROM (revision 16 has none
built in, and boots PROM 2002 for revision 15). The band has no network, so
the session goes over Chaos, `--network chaos`: on revision 16 lispm-check's
default is TCP (section 8 R2), which such a band never answers:

```
tools/microcode-check/ethernet-peer /path/to/peer.sock &
# the wrapper: MUIR_QUUX_REVISION=16, and --ethernet-socket /path/to/peer.sock
# on every run; --prom PROM unless it is a --resume
tools/microcode-check/run ethernet-stray -- --band PACK --ubin UBIN --quux WRAPPER --network chaos
```

| `network-stray` | (`cases-15/`, run only when named, on revision 15; skipped on another) The network's interrupt, word 100 `<6>`, the Chaosnet interface's request, is taken as a stray: the microcode writes the CSR, word 140, 0, which turns both interrupt enables off and drops the level (`INTR-NETWORK-STRAY`, `sys/ucadr/uc-interrupt.lisp`; QUUX's Ethernet contract, section 8, step R3, which took the NCP and `uc-chaos` out). `lisp/network-stray.lisp` writes the CSR through the register page's window: the receive interrupt enable alone, with no packet received, raises no request and stays (the control); the transmit interrupt enable, with Transmit Done up on an idle transmitter, requests at once, and the stray leaves the enables 0 and word 100 `<6>` down. It wants a band without the NCP, on revision 15, which alone has the interface since step R4. Microcode without the stray dismisses the interrupt untouched, and the level interrupts again at once for ever. Its planted control is made from the band's own assembly: `network-stray-plant UBIN PLANT.lisp` writes a file that, given after `lisp/network-stray.lisp` in `--files`, makes the stray a bare dismissal in the running control store; then 3 of its 6 cases fail, the enables kept and word 100 `<6>` up (measured on R3's band4, revision 16; on revision 15, which has no network for a session, its forms were typed at the console with the same results). Until step R3 this row was `chaos-transmit-order`, the Chaosnet's transmit list sent oldest first (`uc-chaos.lisp`'s `CHAOS-XMT-0`); it is retired with the microcode it checked. |
| `range-checks` | The array bounds checks are unsigned (microcode 2001; contract G2 appendix A1.3): `(aref a -1)` and `(aset x a -1)` signal `SUBSCRIPT-OUT-OF-BOUNDS` on general, string, 8-bit and 2-dimensional arrays and write nothing, `array-in-bounds-p` says NIL at -1, `array-push` with a negative fill pointer stores nothing, and `copy-array-portion` with a negative start stores nothing below the array. An index one past the end is the control. |
| `address-in-no-region` | A reference to an address in no region signals `ADDRESS-IN-NO-REGION` (`sys/eh/ehf.lisp`; microcode 2002's `PGF-NO-ENTRY`, `sys/ucadr/uc-page-fault.lisp`), with the address, a locative, and the machine goes on: by `%P-CONTENTS-OFFSET`, `%P-STORE-CONTENTS`, `%P-LDB` and `CAR`, at 2^28 and 2^31; the message names the address; a locative to no region read by `%P-CONTENTS-AS-LOCATIVE` is taken as no oldspace, with no fault; such a pointer held across a process switch and through a full GC. A reference inside a region is the control. It replaces revision 13's `address-past-28-bits`, whose addresses are, from revision 14, in a 32-bit space (its file was `cases/address-past-28-bits.cases`); it needs revision 14. |
| `write-a-memory` | `%WRITE-INTERNAL-PROCESSOR-MEMORIES` and `COMPILER:MA-LOAD-A-MEM` write an A memory location's 40 bits, its tag from `D-HI`'s bits 15:8 (`XWIPM`, `sys/ucadr/uc-cadr.lisp`), read back through A memory's window; location 1777 is restored after. The first case, the location's `<31:0>`, is the control; microcode that writes 32 bits fails the other two. |
| `fixnum-boundaries` | Fixnum arithmetic at the 32-bit boundaries with no sign extension (microcode 2001; contract G2 sections 2.2 and 6.1): `+`, `-`, `*`, `1+`, `1-` and `ASH` at 2147483647 and -2147483648 and their overflow into bignums, the comparisons, `=` and `EQL` on negative fixnums, the one-argument functions, the logical functions, `LDB`, `DPB`, the divisions, a bignum with a negative fixnum operand and characters, interpreted and compiled. Each goes through the type dispatches `D-NUMARG`, `D-NUMARG1` or `D-FIXNUM-NUMARG2`, which fall through for a fixnum, or `ARITH-ANY-FIX` (`sys/ucadr/uc-arith.lisp`). It passes on microcode 2001 before the sign extension went and after. |
| `bignum-fixnum-extremes` | A bignum with -2147483648 and 2147483647 (microcode 2001): `+`, `-`, `*`, `QUOTIENT`, `REMAINDER`, `MOD`, `<`, `>`, `=`, `EQL` and `EQUAL`, then `FLOOR`, `CEILING`, `TRUNCATE`, `ROUND`, `GCD`, `LOGAND`, `LOGIOR`, `LOGXOR`, `MAX`, `MIN` and `CLI://`, the bignum first and the fixnum first, interpreted and compiled, against values computed outside the machine. -2^31's magnitude, 2^31, is not a 32-bit fixnum, so `BFXSUB` and `BFXMPY-OK` (`sys/ucadr/uc-arith.lisp`), which negate the fixnum, take it apart. Microcode without that fails the first three cases, `(- (expt 2 40) most-negative-fixnum)`, `(- (expt 2 31) most-negative-fixnum)` and `(* (expt 2 40) most-negative-fixnum)`, and `(- b f)`, `(* b f)` and `(* f b)` with -2^31 and the remainders made from them. |
| `full-gc` | `SI:GC-IMMEDIATELY` and `SI:FULL-GC` complete (microcode 2001: the page hash table's first words keep their fixnum tag through the selective deposits of `PGF-AG`, `AGER2`, `AGER4` and `XCPGS3`), and a sample of the other checks passes after them. |
| `gcd-setz` | `GCD` of -2^31, the most negative 32-bit fixnum (`GCD-FIX-FIX`, `sys/ucadr/uc-arith.lisp`): with 0 or itself it is the bignum 2^31, and with anything else the gcd of 2^30 and it. `(gcd 12 18)` is the control. |
| `typed-a` | A memory that Lisp reads as a fixnum stays one (microcode 2001; contract G2 section 2.2, an arithmetic result takes M's tag): `ARRAY-PUSH`'s fill pointer (`XFARY-1`, `sys/ucadr/uc-array.lisp`) and `%REGION-CONS-ALARM` and `%PAGE-CONS-ALARM` (`MAKE-REGION`, `uc-storage-allocation.lisp`) after a new region. A fill pointer read before any push is the control. The metering counters (`uc-meter.lisp`) are fixed the same way but not checked, since metering is off. |
| `setz` | +2^31 as a bignum is two 31-bit digits (microcode 2001): negating it, multiplying it by -1, and -2^31's remainder and quotient by it give fixnums (`BIGNUM-MINUS`, `FXBMPY`, `REMAINDER-FIX-BIG`, `FXBIDIV`, `sys/ucadr/uc-arith.lisp`), so the literal -2147483648 reads as the fixnum. -2^31 made by subtraction is the control. |
| `floats` | Floats are IEEE singles (microcode 2001; contract G1 sections 2.3, 2.4): the contract's `(+ 1.0 2.0)`, the rounding of 0.1 and `(float 16777217)`, ties to even, an infinity past the largest single, subnormal operands, 32-bit fixnums and bignums to and from floats, `ART-FLOAT` one word an element, and the Lisp float accessors, limits and `SQRT`. Bits are compared through `%POINTER`. |
| `instance-get` | `GET`, `GETL` and `GET-LOCATION-OR-NIL` of an instance send it the message: `PLGET` marks an instance in the cdr code, `<39:38>` at 40 bits (`uc-fctns.lisp`). A symbol's property is the control. |
| `numbers` | The 40-bit word's numbers (contract G1 section 2.4): 32-bit fixnums near +-2^31 and their overflow into bignums, `ART-32B`, string and 8-bit array words (tag 005), characters, negative subscripts, and a cold-load bignum against a computed one (digits tag 000). Cases marked "fails on 2000" fail on System 2000's band. |
| `apply-large-frame` | `APPLY` stops spreading a list onto the stack at `%SPREAD`'s limit, 370 octal words of frame, and signals its error, `STACK-FRAME-TOO-LARGE` (`UAPLY5`, `sys/ucadr/uc-call-return.lisp`). A frame larger than that wraps the 8-bit deltas of the next call block opened above it (`%%LP-CLS-DELTA-TO-ACTIVE-BLOCK`, `-TO-OPEN-BLOCK`), as an interpreted callee's entry (`INTP1`) opens `APPLY-LAMBDA`'s. `lisp/apply-large-frame.lisp` applies interpreted `&rest` functions, one of them switching stack groups, to N arguments: up to 245 they return N, past it they signal the error and the listener answers afterwards: past 249 `APPLY`'s own, from 246 to 249 the opener's that `INTP1` reaches (`call-block-large-frame`). Microcode without either check returns 250 and 251 and halts the machine at `QMXSG` (`ILLOP`) at 252, where the frame is 256 words, with or without the switch. The functions must stay interpreted: load the file as source, not with `--compile`. |
| `call-block-large-frame` | A call block is opened only below `%SPREAD`'s limit (`CBM0`, `sys/ucadr/uc-macrocode.lisp`; `P3ZERO`, `CALL-BLOCK-TOO-LARGE`, `sys/ucadr/uc-call-return.lisp`): with the new block the frame is at most 370 octal (248) words, counted as `%ASSURE-PDL-ROOM` counts it, so the block's delta to the active block is at most 249; past that the opener signals `STACK-FRAME-TOO-LARGE`, `P3ZERO` after popping the word it pushed. The deltas have 8 bits (`%%LP-CLS-DELTA-TO-ACTIVE-BLOCK`, `-TO-OPEN-BLOCK`), and past 255 they wrapped. `lisp/call-block-large-frame.lisp` opens blocks above large frames three ways: 63, 64 and 100 calls nested in one compiled expression, 4 words of temporaries each (`CBM0`); an `&rest` frame of 233 and 240 arguments with 4 calls nested above it (`CBM0`); and an interpreted function given 246 and 251 arguments by a compiled caller, whose entry opens `APPLY-LAMBDA`'s block (`P3ZERO`); then the depth-64, the 246 and the 240 cases again in a stack group of their own and in a process, which catch the error there. Each signals the error and the listener answers afterwards; 62 calls, 232 and 245 arguments, a delta of 248, 249 and 249, are the controls. Microcode without the check fails all ten error sections: it halts the machine at 64 and 100 calls (`TRAP`'s recursive-error `ILLOP`) and at 240 arguments (`QMMPO2`'s), in a process too, signals a PDL overflow for 64 calls in a stack group, and returns the others. Refusing only past a delta of 255 fails them all as well, five by losing the listener: the error handler's frame (`SG-SAVE-STATE`), pushed where the block would have gone, wraps the same way. A `P3ZERO` without the check fails three, returning 246 and 251. 22 cases, about 15 s on micro. The limit that remains: an interpreted function given 252 arguments or more by a compiled caller is refused, but its listener is lost for that same reason. Load the file as source, not with `--compile`: it compiles all but the interpreted callee. |
| `pdl-refill` | The PDL buffer refill brings a one-q-forward back into the buffer as it was written (`sys/ucadr/uc-page-fault.lisp`, `PB-TRANS`). An interpreted special binding's slot is still the forward, ending its frame, after a process switch, and a `SETQ` after the switch reaches the variable. The first case, with no switch, is the control. Microcode that follows the forward fails the other three. |
| `sg-saved-body-forward` | A stack group's saved accumulator that holds a `DTP-BODY-FORWARD` is restored as saved (`SG-LOAD-BLOCK-INTO-PDL-BUFFER`, `sys/ucadr/uc-stack-groups.lisp`) and left alone by the scavenger (`SCAVL3`, `uc-storage-allocation.lisp`). `STRUCTURE-INFO` leaves one in `M-A` after it measures a structure-forwarded object (`SINFS-HFWD`), and a process switch saves it in the leader; `TRANS-BFWD` takes a body-forward as relative to where it lies, so following it from a leader slot read an unrelated address, or faulted in no region and halted in `ILLOP`. `lisp/sg-saved-body-forward.lisp` puts one in a stack group's saved `M-A`, built so that following it lands on 2^31, in no region. Resuming a stack group that has run once is the control. Microcode 2002 halts at PC 24770 at the resume; with only the resume fixed it halts there in the young collection that copies the stack group. Needs revision 14; it runs interpreted or compiled (`--compile`). |
| `sg-deferred-entry` | A deferred sequence break is the machine's, not the stack group's (`SGENT`, `sys/ucadr/uc-stack-groups.lisp`; QUUX's Ethernet contract, section 2.6): `SGENT` carries the flag `M-DEFERRED-SEQUENCE-BREAK-FLAG` of the group it leaves into the group it enters, where `SB-REINSTATE` asks for the break again, and drops the entered group's saved copy. MIT's restored the entered group's own flag, so a break deferred while the scheduler ran was dropped when it resumed a process. `lisp/sg-deferred-entry.lisp` defers a clock break inside a `WITHOUT-INTERRUPTS` (the bit cleared, then a spin until the clock's break sets it) and enters a stack group in each of `D-SG-ENTER`'s resume states with scheduling allowed there: awaiting initial call (`SG-ENTER-CALL`), awaiting call and resumable (`R-BIT`; resumable is a preempted process the scheduler resumes after the listener waits inside its section), and awaiting return (`QMDDR0`, the caller entered from inside its callee's section). A process at priority 40 counts the scheduler's passes in its wait predicate; the group entered must see a pass between the entry and its first form, and return its own value intact; the resumable case wants two passes or more (MIT's 1): a pass the network process takes first for the session's own frames adds one over TCP (3 on rtl, 5 through the fault relay), and for the case the clock runs at its default rate, 60 ticks, and on a band with the network the Ethernet device's interrupt is disarmed, so that the plant's count stays 1. The pass counter's start is the control. MIT's `SGENT` fails the four entries (measured on revision 15, micro). 7 cases, about 8 s on micro. |
| `extra-pdl-interior` | A locative into the middle of an extra-pdl object is copied out with the object (`EXTRA-PDL-TRAP`, `sys/ucadr/uc-transporter.lisp`). A bignum made by arithmetic lies in `EXTRA-PDL-AREA` until it is stored; a locative to one of its digits, stored into an array or held across a stack-group switch (whose dump of the pdl buffer stores it), trips the write test, and `EXTRA-PDL-TRAP` copies the bignum. It then read the copy's header at the locative's own address, a digit, and `SINFSH` dispatched on that digit's header-type bits: bits that name no header type halted the machine in `ILLOP` (`SINF-FLO`), any other gave a wrong size. `TRANS-COPY` now leaves the copy's first word in `A-TRANS-TEM`, and the header is read there. `lisp/extra-pdl-interior.lisp` takes 2^21 cubed, 2^63, whose second digit is 0; the first case, the bignum in the extra pdl and its digit read through a locative, is the control. Microcode without the change halts at the second case, on micro and rtl. Runs interpreted or compiled: loaded as source, the file compiles its functions, which must keep the bignum on the pdl. |
| `region-area-map-interrupt` | Region-to-area's walk keeps the region it walks for across the interrupts it can take (`REGION-TO-AREA-WALK`, `sys/ucadr/uc-page-fault.lisp`). `REGION-TO-AREA` keeps each region's area in a byte of `A-REGION-AREA-MAP`; a byte that says not yet known makes it walk the region's list thread and keep what it finds. The walk held the region number in `A-TEM1` across its reads, where an interrupt can be taken, and the tick's `TRACK-MOUSE` sets `A-TEM1` to 4 times the mouse's delta, 0 at rest: the area went into region 0's byte, which `generational-tuning`'s `gt-check` found now and then. The region is now kept in `M-A`. `lisp/region-area-map-interrupt.lisp` finds the table in A memory by its contents, then runs timer 0, the tick, at 200 and 50 microseconds while it forgets every byte and looks every region up again (`RGM-STORM`), and checks that no region's byte holds another area's number. The table found, the mouse tracked, the table as left and a storm at the tick's own 16,667 are the controls. Microcode that keeps the region in `A-TEM1` fails the storms on micro and rtl. Runs interpreted or compiled, on revision 14 (measured) and 15. |
| `integer-ops-on-floats` | The integer functions given a single float, a rational, a character or a symbol in each integer position, interpreted and compiled: `GCD`, `\\`, `SYS:INTERNAL-\\`, `LCM`, `\`, `REMAINDER` and `CLI:REM` signal the wrong-type error "a fixnum or a bignum" for a float as for a symbol or a rational (microcode 2001; `ASSURE-BIGNUM`, `sys/ucadr/uc-arith.lisp`, starts the read of the argument only after its type test). A single float is an immediate whose pointer field holds its bits, past the 28-bit address space, and microcode that read memory there first halts at `PGF-MAP-MISS` on the first float case and answers none after it. A character counts as its code; `MOD`, `%DIV`, `LOGAND`, `LOGIOR`, `LOGXOR`, `ASH` and `LDB`, which never reached that code, are there as they answer. Two bignum cases through `ASSURE-BIGNUM` and `(gcd 12 18)` are the controls. |
| `zero-length-region` | Freeing a region of no length leaves the regions around it alone (`sys/ucadr/uc-storage-allocation.lisp`, `FREE-REGION` and `UPDATE-REGION-PHT`). It frees the region of no length the cold load gives `FASL-TEMP-AREA`, as a full GC does, and checks that the region whose first quantum holds its origin still has that quantum in the address space map, that the page below the origin is still in the page hash table, and that `ROOM` still finds every region's area. The first two cases check that the band has such a region. Microcode without the fix answers `(NIL T NIL)` in the third case and fails `ROOM` with "The argument AREA was NIL", or halts at `GET-MAP-BITS`'s "Region not found". |
| `rev14-fiddles` | Revision 14's PDL buffer fiddles against TLB eviction (contract G3 revision 14, section 12's Y2 checks; `lisp/rev14-fiddles.lisp`): a frame of pointers each aliasing its own stack word's TLB index, so that every dump and refill write meets a port-B eviction of its fiddle, dumped by a stack-group switch and refilled (D1); the same under `SET-MAR` on the stack page, status 6 (D2, R1); an extra-PDL bignum above the aliased words (X1); and compiled frames 200 and 300 deep with and without the alias and the MAR. Then the flip keeps the ephemeral-reference bit `<19>`, and register-page word 224 stays 0. The unaliased frames are the controls. Interpreted frames take at most 244 args, 245 spread with the hold's first: past that the hold's entry signals `STACK-FRAME-TOO-LARGE` (`call-block-large-frame`).
